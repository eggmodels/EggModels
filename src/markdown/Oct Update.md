# Why do I (we) choose this for a hobby?
*By: Eugenio Garza Garcia*  
*October 2025*


EggModels is a capital “R” Romantic endeavor. Whenever I talk about our work, I get one of two reactions:
1.	“I have no interest in how you justify your problematic gambling habits,” or
2.	“Wow, that’s awesome. Sounds super cool and interesting. You guys are heroes for fighting the big, bad, inefficient capital flows that pour into the Cowboys every week under the misguided belief that they’re a competent football organization.”

This post is for group two. Not that I expect group one to even click on the link whenever I spam our latest musings across my group chats. But just in case, group one, please go away.

When I describe what we do at EggModels, I always caveat that our work is more academic and recreational than profit-seeking. In my fantasies, EggModels would function like an AI research lab with no business plan, no products, and no pretense of making money. Some VC fund would see this and, in a moment of delusion, hand us billions of dollars to hang out and occasionally build models. Models for models’ sake.

I specifically have this Romantic notion of EggModels’ outputs. Whenever we run our tennis or NFL numbers, I watch the metrics flash across the screen and think: we worked incredibly hard for this 1–2% edge. Late nights, constant monitoring, and endless refinement all leading to small advantages that, pressed consistently over time, produce moderate profits. Proof that our predictions defintionally have value and that our efforts weren’t entirely wasted. There’s a kind of honor in weathering the swings of a season (most of them induced by Kirk Cousins / Stefanos Tsitsipas) and seeing the model prevail.

That was before I discovered Prediction Markets Twitter. I wish I hadn’t but my wallet’s happy I did. Suddenly, I was reading stories of people making money trading prop bets on players ruled out before kickoff, or analyzing the Victoria’s Secret Fashion Show’s “Wonder Bra” history to find riskless arbitrage. Is making money this way honorable? Jacques’ English father would probably not approve. But someone has to help these new markets reach fair value. EggModels will take that burden.

Over the past few months, we’ve been exploring these niche, illiquid markets like mention markets for Taylor Swift’s Life of a Showgirl album. We’ve tested strategies involving third-party data aggregation (including the oboarding of a new Taylor Swift subject matter consultant), legalistic analysis of market rules, and new ways to monitor real-time risk in markets where traditional portfolio optimization doesn’t quite fit.

For example, a few weeks ago our Taylor Swift consultant noticed a Spotify ad in Times Square that revealed lyrics from one of her new songs. Using that intel, we made profitable trades on Kalshi mention markets. The experience raised an important internal question: is it responsible to wager our entire paychecks on the credibility of a Spotify advertisement? We settled on half.

In the coming months, we plan to formalize our learnings and develop a proprietary tool to automate some of these trades, complete with structured risk and ROI metrics. There’s an art to this work a qualitative layer we’re still learning to navigate. Nate Silver’s framework for grading pollsters has inspired us to create our own rubric for evaluating alternative data sources and incorporating them responsibly into our models. We’re still ironing out the details, but we’re excited to share more soon.

Is this as sexy as building random forests to predict tennis matches? Not quite. Is part of this change in direction a jealousy that others were making much more money with far less effort? Probably (thinking of our senior advisor Yousif specifically). But whether the gamification of life is good or bad (probably bad), it’s here to stay and we don’t intend to miss the moment, especially when we feel we already have a head start.
